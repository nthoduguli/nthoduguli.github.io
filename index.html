Building tools to read the genome —
from ancient hominins to single cells.

I'm a rising senior at MIT studying Computer Science & Molecular Biology, with minors in Neuroscience and Political Science. My work sits at the intersection of computational genomics, single-cell transcriptomics, and machine learning at scale — currently in Manolis Kellis's lab at MIT CSAIL, building algorithms and cluster-scale pipelines in Python and C++ to model biological data that doesn't fit on one machine.

01About

I work on problems where genomes, cells, and models meet — reconstructing personalized genomes for ancient and modern hominins, building benchmarks that test whether single-cell language models actually understand biology, and designing generative models that connect cell state to chromatin structure.

Outside the lab, I lead MIT's Biotech Group, run projects for a global health nonprofit erasing medical debt, and TA introductory physics and biological engineering courses.
